# Traceway routing

Geometry comes from the routing engine through `App\Domain\Routing\RoutingEngine`. Production uses Valhalla (`ROUTER_DRIVER=valhalla`, the public server). An LLM never emits coordinates.

- `ValhallaCosting` turns the rider's filters into Valhalla costing options. On foot it is `pedestrian`; on a bike it is `bicycle`.
- Valhalla's pedestrian costing weighs the kind of way (footway, track, living street, a road with a sidewalk), not a road's class or its surface. On foot, Quiet first makes footways and living streets cheaper. The surface sets tracks (Paved neutral, Any a little cheaper, Dirt cheapest), and Dirt makes footways cheaper too. `highway=path` trails get no pull at all. Nothing on foot keeps a route off a busy road or on paved ground, so the copy must not promise it. Check an option against Valhalla's source for the version in `.ddev/commands/host/valhalla` before relying on it.
- A tapped point snaps with `ValhallaCosting::locate()`, which carries the options that decide which ways a route may use (a bike's `avoid_bad_surfaces`, a hike's `max_hiking_difficulty`). Valhalla keeps a road bike on Paved off anything rougher than compacted, a gravel bike on Paved off grass, the roughest tracks and mountain paths, and every bike off steps. The planner's on-screen snap (to a way drawn on the map) and Build points do not know the sport or surface; only the server snap does.
- `Sport::defaultSurface()` is the one source for the surface a sport starts on. The planner gets it as the `sportSurfaces` prop, and the AI connector uses it when the person does not say.
- `RouteCleanliness` ranks loop candidates: no doubled-back road first, then the closest length. `lengthTolerance()` is how far off a loop may be: 15% of a short loop, 500 m × √km of a longer one (5 km on 100 km). `LengthBias` (Close to it, At most, At least) ranks a loop on the wrong side of a most or a least after every other. On Flat or Hilly, each loop's climb above the flattest (or below the hilliest) is added, worth at most one tolerance, so the climb picks among loops near the length and never beats it. The heights come back with the loop's own route request (`elevation_interval`).
- **One judge for every line.** Every choice between lines goes through `RouteCleanliness` (`ranked()`, `cleanest()`, `diverse()`, `flawed()`): a loop, a multisport leg (`CleanLeg` calls it with the earlier legs as `$before`), and every engine's candidates, the OSRM backup's too. Do not score or pick lines anywhere else; add a rule there and it holds for all of them. `tests/Arch/BoundariesTest.php` keeps `LoopShape` behind it.
- A line is flawed when it rides back down a road (its own, or one in `$before`) past the start street, or, as a loop, when `LoopShape::misshapen()` finds a figure of eight, a pinch, a pass back by the pin halfway round, or a stretch beside itself. A flawed line ranks below every clean one.
- `diverse()` offers up to three loops that are not the same line: the clean ones within the tolerance first, then clean ones up to twice the tolerance off. A flawed loop, or one further off, is offered only when nothing else came back, so a rider may see fewer than three. Two loops are the same line when they pass the same places at a quarter, half and three quarters round, so loops through a via point are told apart.
- A multisport loop leg takes the engine's loops and, when every one is flawed against the earlier legs, asks once more with the directions turned 15°.
- `withoutSpurs()` cuts small knots first (a line back within 25 m of where it was, at most 3 km and 15% of the line round), so a lasso becomes a spur and goes too. The engine keeps the line as it was when that would skip the via point.
- A loop goes out through two points on a circle beside the pin, then home. Three points crossed back over each other: 71% of loops round ten Belgian pins were a figure of eight or pinched, against 12% with two.
- `ValhallaRoutingEngine` tries six directions, aimed at `aimMetres()` (half a tolerance inside a most or a least). It aims each direction that came out off the length again, by target ÷ its length, and then tries the six directions between, when fewer than three loops are on the length. At most 18 requests. `RouteQuery::$turnDegrees` turns every direction for Shuffle; the planner steps it by 23°, and the directions repeat every 60°, so 60 shuffles go by before a set comes back. Loop length does not grow smoothly with the circle in every direction (a river or a canal can halve it), so a most or a least can still come back further inside than asked.

Tests for the costing and `RouteQuery` are in `tests/Unit/Domain/Routing`. Record Saloon fixtures for HTTP. Do not call a live router from CI. Measure a costing change on the local server (`ddev valhalla start`), not the public one.
