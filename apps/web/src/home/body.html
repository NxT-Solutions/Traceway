How it works

Three moves. Then you go.

No dragging waypoints around a map. You say where, how far, and which sport. Traceway does the routing.

01

Drop a pin

Your driveway, the trailhead, the park. The loop starts and ends right there, on foot or on a bike.

02

Name the distance and the sport

40 km on the road. 12 km on foot. Each sport scores the map its own way, so the same pin gives a different loop.

03

Take it with you

Export a GPX for a watch or GPS, or a FIT course for Garmin. The device draws the line on its own map. The phone can stay at home.

Multisport

Swim, ride, run. One plan.

Put several sports in one plan. Every leg gets its own sport, length, and colour, and the legs meet at your transition. Rehearse a race at its real distances, or meet friends at a handover with your gear and each cover your own distance.

Loops or a chain

Every leg can leave the transition and come back to it, so you change once and go again. Or chain the legs, so each one ends where the next begins. Give a chained leg a length and it winds out to cover it on the way to the next handover.

No leg turns back on its own road, or runs back over a road an earlier leg used.

On the device

Leave the phone at home.

Traceway writes a GPX file for a watch, a bike computer, or any GPS that reads one, and a FIT course for Garmin. Import it in Connect under Training, then Courses, and sync. Over USB, drop the FIT in Garmin/NewFiles. The device follows the line on its own map. No signal required.

A Garmin reads the first 15 characters of a course name, so every file carries a short name beside the full one. You never see a course called "morning-loop-westerlo-via-be".

A multisport plan gives one FIT course for each leg on land, named like "Brick 2 Run", ready to load into its leg of a multisport activity. The GPX holds every leg as its own track.

No feed.No club.No public routes.

Traceway is a solo planner. Your pin, your loop and your Sunday morning stay yours. Nobody sees where you start, nobody ranks your day, and the only thing that leaves the planner is a course file, headed for your watch or GPS.

There is nothing to scroll and nobody to keep up with. You get a line that is worth the day, and the quiet to take it. The map is OpenStreetMap. The judgement about what makes a road or a path good is ours.

Questions

Before you drop the pin.

Does the device need a signal or an app?

No. The course file is the whole outing. Load it once, over sync or USB, and the watch or GPS follows the line on its own map, with no phone and no signal.

FIT or GPX, which one do I pick?

FIT for a Garmin watch or bike computer, because it carries turn guidance and the short course name the device expects. GPX for COROS, Suunto, Wahoo, Hammerhead, and almost anything else. Both files hold the same line.

Can I make the loop pass a particular place?

Add a via point. The loop bends through it and stays a loop: closed, on roads, never doubling back along the same stretch. The distance updates as it bends.

Can I plan a triathlon or a brick?

Yes. Open Multisport in the planner and pick a brick, a triathlon, a duathlon, or an aquabike. Choose a race distance, from a super sprint to a full Ironman, or set each leg yourself. Every leg on land gets its own FIT course.

Does Traceway route the swim?

No. A routing engine cannot route on water, so you draw the swim yourself, point by point on the lake, or drop a single pin. The planner shows how much you have drawn against the race distance.

Can friends meet me at a handover?

Yes. Chain the legs and set each handover on the map, then give each leg the length you want. Drag a handover to move it, and the plan draws again.

Can I follow the route before I go?

Every route has a player under the map. Slide it from the start to the finish, or press play, and a pin follows the line in the colour of its leg.

Will it send me down a busy road?

Each sport has its own idea of a good line. Road, walk, and run score busy roads down hard, so they lose early. Gravel, mountain bike, and trail care more about surface and climb than about traffic.

Does it work outside Belgium?

The map is OpenStreetMap, so the pin goes anywhere the map has roads and paths. The loops shown on this page leave Westerlo, where Traceway is built.
